## Three places an org describes itself
| | What it publishes | What others publish | What it must publish |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Sources | Site, newsroom, leadership pages, annual report | News coverage, Wikipedia, Crunchbase, trade press, court records | Filings, budgets, procurement awards, contract registers |
| Reliability | First-party claims — check each date, seek independent support for anything important | Less controlled, covers what the org wouldn't raise itself | Can name suppliers, systems, values, and dates self-published material won't |

Starting points: passhe.edu-style institutional sites, aleph.occrp.org (leaked/public records search), crunchbase.com.

Corroboration = support from independent additional evidence. Several copies of one claim, repeated across sites, are not independent evidence.

## Organizational change
An acquisition or merger adds domains, networks, and inherited systems to the review — and pre-existing compromises can come along with it:
- **Marriott/Starwood** — Starwood's reservation system was already compromised before the 2016 acquisition closed. Marriott didn't disclose it until Nov 2018 (initially ~500M guests, later revised to <383M). UK regulator fine: ~£18M.
- **Verizon/Yahoo** — Yahoo disclosed two historic breaches *during* the Verizon acquisition; Verizon cut the price by $350M, closing at $4.48B.

**A public proposal is not a completed merger.** Check for a dated completion record before treating two orgs as merged — a proposed consolidation can be revised or dropped (PASSHE's 2020 proposals for Slippery Rock changed twice before the final PennWest/Commonwealth mergers took effect in 2022 — Slippery Rock itself was never included in the version that went through).

## Staffing
Job postings name systems and the teams that use them:
- An admin posting names the product, often the version, and the owning team
- A migration posting names what's being moved away from, and to
- Professional networks add shape: team sizes, reporting lines, tenure

**Why it comes first:** a vendor named in a job advert is a system to go look for — postings can describe infrastructure before it ever shows up in a technical record. Search the careers site and any state/public jobs portal directly, not just professional networks.
